# Glossary

## Clann

The wider connected group supported by Eolas. A Clann contains people whose
lives or responsibilities are connected, whether or not they are related or
live together.

## Person

An individual whose life is directly represented in the Clann's records. A
person can belong to one or more households over time and does not need to be
related to other Clann people by blood or marriage.

## Household

A physical home or practical living unit within a Clann. A Clann can contain
multiple households, and a household can include people who are not family.

## Membership

A person's association with a household. A membership records details such as
the person's practical household role (such as householder, family, carer, or
lodger) and residence status independently of their age and
relationships with other people. Terms such as child and parent describe
relationships, not household roles.

## Relationship

The connection between two people, such as spouse, parent, child, carer, or
emergency contact. Relationships are recorded independently of household
membership.

## Contact

A person or organisation the Clann may need to contact but whose life is not
directly represented by the Clann. Examples include a neighbour, executor, or
emergency contact who is not otherwise a Clann person.

## Professional

A person, practice, or organisation providing a professional service to the
Clann, such as a GP, solicitor, accountant, or insurer. Professionals are kept
separate from Clann people and general contacts.

## Shared resource

A record or resource that belongs to the Clann as a whole rather than to one
person or household.

## Primary person

The principal record owner or person responsible for the Clann's Eolas
records. This is an administrative role and does not imply a family
relationship or household status.

## Primary household

The initial or principal household for a Clann. The first release creates one
primary household, while the data model permits additional households later.

## Resident

A person whose current household membership has the `resident` status. A
person can remain part of the Clann while living elsewhere and therefore not
being a resident of its primary household.

## CLI functional area

A positional command selecting a functional area of Eolas. For example,
`clann` selects Clann operations and `log` selects log operations.

## CLI action parameter

An option selecting the operation to perform on an entity. For example,
`--create` starts the creation wizard. Future actions may include `--update`,
`--delete`, and `--print`.
This glossary explains words used in clanneolas.com in plain language. It is a
starting point, not legal, medical or financial advice. Meanings and procedures
can differ between England and Wales, Scotland, Northern Ireland and other
countries. Follow current guidance for the place and circumstances involved and
seek qualified advice when necessary.

## Administrator

A person authorised to deal with the estate of someone who died when there is
no executor able or willing to act. In England, Wales and Northern Ireland, an
administrator is normally appointed through letters of administration. In
Scotland, the court-appointed role is commonly called an *executor dative*.

See also: [executor](#executor), [personal representative](#personal-representative).

## Attorney

In this handbook, an attorney is a person legally appointed to make, or help
with, specified decisions for someone else under a power of attorney. Their
authority is limited by the document and the law that applies. Here, *attorney*
does not mean a lawyer.

The types of power of attorney and when an attorney may act differ across UK
jurisdictions. For example, an England and Wales lasting power of attorney is
not the same legal instrument as those used in Scotland or Northern Ireland.

See [GOV.UK: Make, register or end a lasting power of attorney](https://www.gov.uk/power-of-attorney/overview).

## Beneficiary

A person or organisation entitled to receive money, property or another benefit
from a will, estate, trust, insurance policy or similar arrangement. The
entitlement depends on the particular document and applicable law; being a
beneficiary does not normally make someone responsible for administering the
estate.

## Capacity

The ability to make a particular decision at the time it needs to be made. A
person may be able to make one decision but not another, and their capacity may
change. An unwise choice alone does not necessarily mean that a person lacks
capacity.

Legal tests and terminology differ by jurisdiction. Do not use the handbook to
make or record a formal capacity assessment.

See [GOV.UK: Mental capacity in lasting power of attorney guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/lasting-power-of-attorney-forms/lp12-make-and-register-your-lasting-power-of-attorney-a-guide-web-version#what-is-mental-capacity).

## Confirmation

In Scotland, the legal document that gives an executor authority to receive and
make payments on behalf of an estate. It is broadly comparable in purpose—not
identical—to a grant of representation elsewhere in the UK.

See [Scottish Government: The executors](https://www.gov.scot/publications/death-scotland-practical-advice-times-bereavement-revised-11th-edition-2016-9781786522726/pages/12/).

## Estate

The money, property, possessions, rights and debts that must be considered when
a person dies. Not every asset associated with the person will necessarily form
part of their estate; ownership, nominations, trusts and jurisdiction can affect
the position.

## Executor

A person responsible for administering the estate of someone who has died. An
executor identifies estate assets and debts, deals with tax and other
liabilities, obtains legal authority where required and distributes what
remains to those entitled to receive it.

In England, Wales and Northern Ireland an executor is normally appointed by a
will. Scottish law uses *executor* for the person administering an estate,
including an *executor dative* appointed when necessary by a court.

See [HMRC definitions for estates](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/international-manual/intm340020) and [Scottish Government executor guidance](https://www.gov.scot/publications/death-scotland-practical-advice-times-bereavement-revised-11th-edition-2016-9781786522726/pages/12/).

## Grant of representation

A court-issued document giving the named person authority to administer an
estate. In England and Wales this is an umbrella term that includes a grant of
probate and grants of letters of administration. Northern Ireland also uses
grant of representation, probate and letters of administration terminology.
Not every estate requires a grant.

Scotland generally uses *confirmation* instead.

See [GOV.UK: Grant of representation](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/technical-guidance-for-official-receivers/56-deceased-insolvents#grant-of-representation) and [nidirect: Probate](https://www.nidirect.gov.uk/articles/probate).

## Intestacy

The legal situation when someone dies without a valid will disposing of their
estate. Intestacy rules determine who may administer and inherit the estate;
those rules vary by jurisdiction and family circumstances.

## Next of kin

A commonly used description for a close relative or a person nominated as an
emergency contact. In health and care settings it may identify whom staff should
contact or consult.

Being described as next of kin does **not** by itself give a person legal
authority to make health, welfare or financial decisions, or a general right to
access records. Authority must come from the applicable law or a valid legal
arrangement, such as a relevant power of attorney or court appointment.

Do not confuse *next of kin* with *nearest relative*, which is a specific role
under mental health legislation in some jurisdictions.

See [NHS guidance on next of kin](https://www.fhft.nhs.uk/patients-and-visitors/patient-information-library/next-kin) and [GOV.UK guidance on nearest relatives](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/information-for-nearest-relatives-mental-health-tribunal-t117/information-for-nearest-relatives).

## Personal representative

A general term for a person legally responsible for administering an estate. In
England, Wales and Northern Ireland this may be an executor or administrator.
Terminology differs in Scotland, where the person administering the estate is
called an executor.

## Probate

The legal process concerned with establishing authority to administer a
deceased person's estate. The word is also commonly used more broadly for the
whole process of dealing with an estate.

More precisely, in England, Wales and Northern Ireland a *grant of probate* is
issued to an executor named in a valid will. Other circumstances may require
letters of administration. Scotland uses a different process called
*confirmation*.

## Will

A legal document recording how a person wants relevant property and possessions
dealt with after death and, depending on the jurisdiction, other appointments
or wishes. The rules for making and interpreting a valid will vary. A handbook
may record where a current will is held, but it is not a substitute for the
will itself.
## Phase 1 shared-domain terms

**Absent** — A fact was not provided or is not present. It is distinct from a
confirmed unknown value and from not applicable.

**Aggregate** — The smallest consistency boundary whose owner validates and
changes it atomically.

**Authority** — Dated, evidenced permission for one party to act for another
within a jurisdiction, activation condition, scope and restrictions.

**Authority Registration** — A provider's separate operational recognition of
an Authority; it is not proof that the underlying Authority exists or is active.

**Continuity Dependency** — An explained directed relationship showing that
one canonical record relies on, funds, supports or otherwise affects another.

**Continuity role** — A dated, module-owned description of how a record matters
to household continuity, such as a primary operating relationship or essential
service. It does not imply ownership or authority and its vocabulary belongs to
the module publishing it.

**Evidence Reference** — A classified, checksum-backed secure pointer to an
immutable original. Evidence supports knowledge but is not automatically fact.

**Identifier** — A typed external reference with masked display and optional
protected value. It never identifies an Eolas aggregate.

**Not applicable** — A fact deliberately does not apply to the subject; it is
not missing and must not be treated as unknown.

**Observation** — A value known as of a particular time with provenance and a
confirmation or estimation status.

**Obligation** — A responsibility owned by its domain module. Stopping its
payment arrangement does not settle or cancel it.

**Payment arrangement** — A future Banking-owned relationship describing how
an obligation is paid. It is planned for Phase 2 and is not yet implemented.

**Provenance** — The source, time, actor and derivation context of knowledge.

**Record identity** — A stable opaque Clann-scoped ID with aggregate type and
one owner module; it is never a name, filename, slug or provider reference.

**Unknown** — The fact applies but its value is not known.
