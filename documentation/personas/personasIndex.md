# Personas

Personas are fictional, evidence-seeking archetypes used to keep decisions
focused on people rather than features. They are not example household records
and must never be based on an identifiable person without an appropriate
research and consent process.

Each feature requirement links to at least one persona and explains the user
need it addresses. Reviewers should ask whether the proposed outcome would
still help that person under the stress, time pressure, accessibility needs and
technology constraints described.

The initial personas are deliberately varied but are not exhaustive:

- [PER-001: Sarah](sarah.md) needs practical guidance after her partner dies
  unexpectedly.
- [PER-002: David](david.md) wants to prepare useful information while living
  independently from distant family.
- [PER-003: Amina](amina.md) coordinates care after her mother loses capacity.
- [PER-004: Morgan](morgan.md) wants to prepare a handbook without exposing their
  household's private data.

## Using and maintaining personas

- Link personas by ID and path from requirements, ADRs and research notes.
- Use them to test assumptions, not to claim that fictional stories are user
  research.
- Add or revise personas when real, ethically gathered evidence reveals an
  unmet situation.
- Avoid stereotypes and irrelevant demographic details.
- Keep all names and circumstances explicitly fictional and do not include
  addresses, identifiers, account details or credentials.
